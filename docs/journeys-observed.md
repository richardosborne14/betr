# Three people walk in off Instagram — what they did, and where they nearly left

> **These are not real users.** They are three characters written by the same person who wrote
> the app, walked through it in a real browser on a 390-wide screen. That is the weakest kind
> of evidence there is and it must be labelled that way wherever it is quoted. Nothing here is
> a testimonial: MHRA treats a testimonial as an implied claim (research §5.2).
>
> What it catches is the class of problem that fails **on sight** — the gluten problem. It
> cannot catch anything subtler, and it is not a substitute for the CBT reviewer, Misha's read,
> or a screen-reader pass. See `docs/tasks/B21-three-people-walk-in.md`.

**Walked 2026-09-04**, against the working tree including that day's uncommitted wording change
(new front screen, the line on the blank box, the grammar nudge). Headless Chrome, 390 × 844 at
3×, `Europe/London`, storage cleared before each walk. Driven with `tools/walk.js`, one command
per step, so no step knew what the next screen held.

**Entry, identical for all three:** they saw someone on Instagram post that they were at **6 out
of 10** on a belief about their anxiety. They are intrigued and cautious, on a phone, one tap
from closing the tab.

## Dan

**1 · Landing page.** *"That's not what I saw. The post had a number going down; this is a
sentence. 'Played it out a hundred times' — played what out? …Oh. The thing in your head.
Yeah. 'No account, no AI' — everyone says that. Where's the catch."*
**Does not tap the big button.** Goes looking for the price.

**2 · Help.** *"Whoa. I asked what this is and it's opened with a suicide line."* Scrolls. Four
thousand pixels of it. *"Nothing about money except — hang on. 'TrybeUP … the private groups
need a paid plan.' They've told me their own other app is paywalled, in the small print, about
a thing they're not even selling me here. Nobody does that."*
**Stays.** Backs out and starts.

**3 · Doors.** *"'Drink, weed, porn, betting.' First one, no dancing around it. That's me. —
'If you're dependent on alcohol or drugs, this isn't the right thing.' Am I? No. Don't think
so."* Taps door one.

**4 · The worries behind it.** *"Hang on. It said porn on the last screen. These are all about
going to the pub. 'Turning up and not joining in'? I haven't got a turning-up problem. —
'Telling someone I'm struggling.' …I've never said it to anyone. Alright. That one."*

**5 · Which of the three.** *"'Pick the one that would sting.' First one. They'll think less of
me. That's exactly it."* Instant, no hesitation.

**6 · The test.** *"'Don't finish it with but I'm fine.' …How do they know I do that."*
Locks in.

**7 · The install card.** *"Here it comes, this is where it asks for something. …No. It's
telling me why. Safari deletes it."* Then: *"I'm not putting an icon called BETR on my home
screen where my girlfriend can see it."* **Not now.**

**8 · That evening.** Types what happened. Re-rates **a bit less sure** — cautious, first go.

**9 · Result. 10 → 9.** *"Down one. One rung. The bloke on Instagram was at six, so that's
what, a month of this? …'The second one is where it starts to stick.' Alright. It's not
overselling it. Nothing else has ever not oversold it."*

**Finished.** Cautiously in.

## What it cost, most expensive first

### 1 · Door one is the first thing everybody reads, and it says *porn*
**Priya nearly left here, on screen two.** She came for an anxiety ladder and the app's second
screen leads with drink, weed, porn and betting. She stayed only because she scrolled. Half the
people BETR could serve are in doors five and six, and both are below the fold.
**One line to change: the order of `items` in `whats-going-on.js`.** It is a real trade —
Marcus and Dan both found themselves in door one immediately, and putting it fourth costs them
the recognition that kept them. **Founder's and Misha's call, not a fix to apply quietly.**

### 2 · We tell Marcus to go somewhere that isn't there
The note says *"If you're dependent on alcohol or drugs, this isn't the right thing. Help has
places that are."* **Help has no alcohol or drug service on it at all** — `places.js` is CBT
worksheets, therapist registers, Mind, Side by Side and TrybeUP. The one sentence in BETR that
sends somebody away sends them to a screen that does not have what it promised. Either the note
stops promising, or `places.js` gains the services it names. **This is a promise the app breaks
today, and it breaks it for the most vulnerable person who reads it.**

### 3 · The door names an audience the list underneath doesn't serve
Door one says *porn*. Of the five worries behind it, three are shaped like a pub — turning up
and not joining in, leaving before everyone else, sitting still. Dan noticed within seconds.
Research §08 is explicit that porn recovery is a **distinct** mechanic (accountability-partner
requests run 9.53 per 1,000 posts against 0.34 in the alcohol subs, across 95,707 posts). Either
the door stops saying porn or the list gains worries a man in porn recovery recognises.

### 4 · Nothing on the way in shows the thing that brought them
All three arrived from a 6/10 screenshot. The ladder first appears **after a completed test** —
seven screens and one real-world action later. The landing page has no number on it at all.

### 5 · The front screen no longer says why everything starts at 10 — *and that is today's change*
Scope §3: *"Everything starts at 10 — that is what the front screen says."* The old headline
**Sure it'll go badly?** carried it. **You've played it out a hundred times** does not. Dan hit
his 9 with no frame for it and read it as barely moving. **This is a regression introduced on
2026-09-04 and it was not spotted until the walk.**

### 6 · Help opens with a crisis helpline, and the proof is 2,711px down
Dan tapped Help to find the price. The entire first screen is *If you are in danger or in
crisis*. The counters that would have won him — **0 accounts · 0 B sent to us, ever** — sit
**2,711px** into a **4,112px** page, three and a half screens of scrolling on an 844px phone.
The crisis block is first for good reasons (B17) and nobody should move it without saying why.
But the person tapping *Help* to check the price and the person in crisis are not the same
person, and right now they get the same screen.

### 7 · "Didn't get to it" barely changes anything
The heading still says **LOCKED IN · Go and do it**, and both buttons stay. Marcus was not sure
it had registered. Rule 5 is honoured in the words and undercut by the screen.

### 8 · The new boundary line is on one of the two free-text screens
*"Not the weather, and not your body"* shows on the blank box reached from the doors. It does
**not** show on *I'll put it my own way* under a stock worry — which is the path Priya took, and
a path a person can reach in four taps. `beliefOwn()` does not pass `foot`.

### 9 · Two worries on one screen share an identical second half
Adjacent, under door one: `early` — *"If I leave early, **then it costs me something with
them**"* — and `strug` — *"If I let someone see I'm struggling, **then it costs me something
with them**"*. Word for word. One for the CBT reviewer's list, next to `strug`/`low` and
`care`/`praise`.

### 10 · The install card protects the data and exposes the person
*"I'm not putting an icon called BETR on my home screen."* Home-screen install is the answer to
Safari's seven-day eviction (research §9.1) and it is the one thing this audience has a reason
to refuse. Worth knowing before B5.

## What worked, and should be left alone

- **B20's screen.** *"Pick the one that would sting."* All three chose instantly and without
  re-reading. The extra tap it cost is bought back on this screen alone.
- **The safety-behaviour line is the best sentence in the product.** *"Don't hold a glass as
  cover."* *"Don't finish it with 'but I'm fine'."* Both landed as the app knowing something
  the person had never said out loud. Clark & Wells (research §2.3) working exactly as written.
- **Rule 4 held.** Marcus's worry is about drinking and his test never touches the drink.
- **Rule 5 held.** No streak, no red day, *"smaller counts, too."*
- **The verdict reframe worked on a sympathetic person.** Priya was refused, understood why in
  one read, and rewrote it herself without help.
- **Nothing was requested after load**, on any screen, in any of the three walks. The tripwire
  in `tools/walk.js` records every `fetch` and `XMLHttpRequest`; it stayed empty throughout.
- **Being told about TrybeUP's paywall in the small print bought trust rather than costing it.**
  Dan's word: *"nobody does that."* Rule 9 is earning its keep.

## What these walks could not test

Today's **grammar nudge never fired.** Both sentences typed into a box were conditionals, so the
loosening that 2026-09-04 was mostly about went unexercised — as did the wall it replaced. That
is a fair result and not a good one: it means the change is still only proved by its tests.

And nobody here has a screen reader, a low-end phone, big text turned on, or a worry the
twenty-one do not cover. Three characters written by the author of the app cannot find what the
author cannot imagine.
