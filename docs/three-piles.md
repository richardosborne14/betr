# The three piles — every sentence in BETR, sorted

**For the founder and Misha. Written 2026-09-04 (B22). Nothing in the app was changed to make
this.** It sorts and proposes; the words are yours.

> **Decided, 2026-09-04, later the same day.** The founder took proposals **2, 4 and 5** as
> written and they are live. **1 and 3 are still open** — 1 is Misha's casting vote inside B23,
> and 3 wants a second opinion on the legal point before it moves. Each entry below is marked.
> The "Now" text under 2, 4 and 5 is what the app said *before* the change, kept because it is
> the evidence for why it moved.

## The rule this sorts by

> **Describe the inside of the moment. Never name the kind of person.**

It comes from the three walks (`docs/journeys-observed.md`). Every moment that worked described
the inside of a moment. Every moment that nearly lost somebody named a kind of person. A
description asks *is that me?*, which a person answers in half a second. A category asks *am I
one of those?*, which is the question this audience has spent years answering defensively.

**It is a rule about naming kinds of people, not about hard words.** "Behavioural experiment"
is a hard phrase and nobody bounced off it. "Drink, weed, porn, betting" is four easy words and
one person nearly closed the tab.

## How it came out

BETR puts **487 sentences** in front of a person. They sort like this:

| Pile | What it is | How many | What happens to it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | Describes the inside of a moment | **465** | Nothing. This is the model for anything rewritten |
| **2** | Names a category, and has to | **17** | Stays, word for word. The only question is *where it sits* |
| **3** | Names a category, and doesn't have to | **5** | The work. Five proposals below, all yours to take or leave |

Pile 1 is everything not named in piles 2 and 3 below. That includes the whole worry list bar
one sentence, all six door labels, all 21 "Why this one sticks" explanations, every screen of
the loop, and everything a screen reader says.

| Where | The sentence | What they said |

## Pile 3 — names a category, and doesn't have to

**Five sentences. None of them has been changed.** Each one below is what it says now, what it
costs, and one proposed replacement.

### 1 · Door one's line — and it is four words, not the whole line — **STILL OPEN (B23)**

**Now:** *"The one you've quietly decided to stop more than once, and haven't — **drink, weed,
porn, betting**. BETR never goes near the thing itself. Only what you think happens if people
see you without it."*

**What it cost:** Priya read this on screen two and nearly closed the tab — *"Is this a recovery
app? That's not me at all."*

**What this pass adds:** it is **not the label** and **not the safety note**. The label
(*"Something I keep doing more than I mean to"*) is a description and one of the best on the
screen. It is the four nouns in the middle. Take them out and the sentence still describes the
same moment:

> *"The one you've quietly decided to stop more than once, and haven't. BETR never goes near
> the thing itself — only what you think happens if people see you without it."*

**But it is a real trade and it is not mine to take.** Those four words are exactly why Dan and
Marcus found themselves in half a second: *"First one, no dancing around it. That's me."*
**This belongs to B23** — the same decision as the order of the doors, and Misha's casting vote.
B22's contribution is only to say precisely which four words did the damage.

### 2 · "Which one?" — the line under every worry list — **TAKEN, LIVE**

**Now:** *"Not here on purpose: anything that tests **the drink, the screen or the habit
itself**. Those aren't tests. We test the worry underneath."*

**What it costs:** this is on the screen every single person reaches, whichever door they came
through. It is the second time Priya meets recovery language, and she has none of those three
things. The boundary it draws is right and has to stay; the three nouns are what carries it.

**Proposed:**

> *"Nothing here tests the thing itself, only what you expect to happen without it. That's the
> worry underneath, and that's what we test."*

Says the same to Marcus, says nothing about Priya.

### 3 · The footer on "What's going on?" — **STILL OPEN**

**Now, as a person reads it** (two lines are printed together):
*"None of these gets tested. The worry underneath does. **None of these is a diagnosis, and
BETR never decides which one you are.**"*

**What it costs:** it puts the word *diagnosis* in front of somebody who had not thought of one.
The first half does the whole job the screen needs.

**Worth knowing before you decide:** the disclaimer Google Play requires is frozen sentence 1
— *"It does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition"* — and that lives in Help. The
UK regulator is explicit that a general disclaimer is not what protects a product anyway; what
protects it is making no claim, which the doors don't. **So this line is an extra rather than a
requirement.** I am not a lawyer and that is worth a second opinion before it moves.

**Proposed:**

> *"None of these gets tested. The worry underneath does. Nothing here is decided for you."*

### 4 · The card sentence on "Resting when there's stuff to do" — **TAKEN, LIVE**

**Now:** *"If I rest before everything's done, **then I'm being lazy**."*

**Two problems, and the second is the interesting one.** It names a kind of person. And it is a
verdict rather than a prediction — which is the exact thing BETR refuses when somebody types
one. Priya typed *"I'm not as good at this as they think I am"* and was told *"That's a verdict,
not a prediction."* She took it well. But a stranger reading the worry list meets BETR doing the
thing it tells them off for.

**On the record:** this sentence was already softened once, from *"I'm worthless"* to *"I'm
being lazy"* (scope §5.3). So this is a second look at a sentence somebody has already thought
about.

**Nothing breaks either way** — this is the loose sentence on the card, and it is never itself
tested. The three underneath it are all proper predictions and none of them is affected.

**Proposed:**

> *"If I rest before everything's done, then I'll pay for it."*

Same moment, no verdict, and still loose enough to be the card rather than one of the three.

### 5 · The refusal, when somebody's own test involves the thing itself — **TAKEN, LIVE**

**Now:** *"That test involves the thing itself. Those aren't tests — the worry underneath is.
Try one about what people will think, or about going without **the crutch**."*

**What it costs:** small, but it is BETR putting a judging word on the person's thing at the
moment it has just told them no.

**Proposed:**

> *"...Try one about what people will think, or about what happens when you go without it."*

## Three things this pass found and deliberately did not sort

- **Hard words are a different problem, and not a big one.** CBT, behavioural experiment, safety
  behaviour, the nine sentences. They name a method, not a kind of person. Nobody in the walks
  bounced off any of them. They stay.
- **The weight of Help.** Dan scrolled 4,112 pixels of correct, careful, heavy words to find one
  thing. Nothing on that screen is wrong. All of it arrives at once. That is a question of order,
  not wording — **B26**.
- **What actually bought the sceptic.** Dan's reaction to *"No account, no AI, nothing leaves
  your phone"* was *"everyone says that."* What kept him was the small print admitting TrybeUP's
  own paywall — *"nobody does that."* The promise did not win him; the admission did. Worth
  knowing before anybody writes another promise line.
